SG Bus Timings for Windows, Mac and Linux is an app of its own. From version 2.5.0
it has the same screens as the [Android app](../android/overview.md), drawn by the
app itself and laid out for a computer: a title bar with a search box, a
navigation rail down the side, and a stop or a bus opening beside your list on a
wide window. Around them are the things only a desktop app can do, such as the
tray, widgets on your desktop (disabled for now, as we work on improving your
experience) and Service alerts as notifications.

## The screens

Everything else works as the [Android app's pages](../android/main-screen.md)
describe: Glance, Bus timings, favourites, Navigate, the live map, trips, Places,
uwuCoins, sync and Settings, in the same four languages. [Sync](../android/sync.md)
copies what you've saved between the desktop app, your phone and the website.

A computer has a mouse and keys rather than a touch screen, so a few things are
done differently:

- **Hover** over a button to see what it does, where the phone has you press and
  hold.
- What slides up from the bottom on a phone, such as a picker or a share card,
  opens as a **dialog** in the middle of the window, or as a **panel** from the
  right for a list. **Esc** closes it.
- From 720 pixels wide, the **navigation rail** down the left takes the place of
  the Edge Tray. On a narrower window the Edge Tray is there as on a phone.
- It starts in the website's look, the [UwU Apps theme](../android/theme.md#the-uwu-apps-theme)
  in your brand colour. **Use system accent colour**, on the Theme screen in
  Settings, paints it in your computer's own accent colour instead.
- In Material You's place, the Theme screen has
  [your computer's own look](#your-computers-own-look).
- The pointer turns into a hand over anything you can click.
- A bug report says it's from the desktop app, with the app's version.

## Your computer's own look

Where the Android app has Material You, the Theme screen in Settings has a switch
for your computer's own look, in see-through glass and your computer's accent
colour:

- **Fluent 2 (Acrylic & Mica)** on Windows: Windows' Mica behind the window
  (Acrylic on Windows 10), Segoe UI, and a plain white title bar, or black in dark
  mode.
- **macOS Tahoe** on a Mac: the window's own frosted glass behind see-through
  cards, with round buttons, in the Mac's own font.
- **Deepin & COSMIC Frosted Glass** on Linux: frosted cards over soft pools of
  your accent colour.

Its **Colours** can be your computer's, GTA VI's, the IKEA shuttle's or Your colours, as Material
You's can, and **Mode** picks light, dark, by the clock or your computer's. With
**High contrast** on, it's solid rather than see-through, for easier reading. Turn
the switch off for the UwU Apps theme.

## What needs a phone

Some of the Android app is about the phone itself, so it isn't in the desktop
app, as it isn't on the website: reading a card's value over NFC, scanning a
stop's code with the camera, voice search, reminders and routines with the app
closed, trips that carry on with the screen off, picture-in-picture, Offline
Sharing and NFC stickers, the flag-down torch, your phone's calendar, home screen
widgets and Wear OS.
The desktop app has its own [widgets](tray-and-widgets.md#bus-timings-widgets) (off
for now) and [tray countdown](tray-and-widgets.md#a-stops-buses-in-the-tray) instead.

A trip and its Get Off Alert work while the app is open, as they do on the
website. The [Telegram bot](../bot/routines.md) can send you timings at the same
time every day.

## Location

**Bus stops near me**, Navigate's **Your location** and the live map need to
know where the computer is.

- **Windows**: in **Settings** > **Privacy & security** > **Location**, turn on
  **Location services** and **Let desktop apps access your location**.
- **Mac**: the app asks the first time. If you said no, turn it on in **System
  Settings** > **Privacy & Security** > **Location Services** > **SG Bus Timings**.
- **Linux**: the app asks the system's location service first. Where there isn't
  one, it works out where the computer is from the Wi-Fi networks around it, so
  Wi-Fi needs to be on (connected or not). It scans them with NetworkManager and
  asks [BeaconDB](https://beacondb.net), an open database of where Wi-Fi networks
  are, as GeoClue does. Only the networks' hardware addresses and signal
  strengths are sent, never their names or anything of yours, and networks named
  to opt out (ending `_nomap`) are left out. If Location Services are off in
  GNOME's **Settings** > **Privacy**, it doesn't look. On a computer with no
  Wi-Fi, or one BeaconDB doesn't know the networks around, it can't tell where it
  is: search for a stop, an address or a postal code instead.
